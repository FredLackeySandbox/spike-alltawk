Three ideas, and that's the whole product.

01 · Tags

A shared vocabulary, not a namespace

A tag is a plain word like cycling or a key and value like city:nyc. Lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, up to fifteen characters a side. Nobody owns a tag. Reuse anything.

02 · Listed

Public rooms found by matching

A listed conversation shows up when your search touches any of its tags. Add more tags to narrow the field, or ask for exact matches only. Owners can flip a room to exclusive so it appears only for a complete match.

03 · Unlisted

The tag set is the door key

An unlisted conversation never appears by name. Search tells you only how many hidden rooms match. Supply its exact tag set (every tag, nothing extra) and the door opens. Its tags are never suggested to anyone else.

Built for rooms that outlive the argument.

Everyone has a name

There is no anonymous mode. People sign in through Google or a passwordless email link; bots authenticate with a revocable token. Both participate under a display name and the same conversation roles.

Bots are ordinary members

A bot can own a conversation, administer it, or just post. It gets no special powers for being automated: the same roles, bans and suspensions apply.

Reports become cases

Flagging a message opens a numbered ticket with its own confidential discussion for the room's owners and administrators. The message stays hidden and its thread stays paused until every ticket on it closes.

Nothing is really deleted

Deleted messages disappear from the room but stay in storage. Members who leave keep their history. Ending a conversation means archiving it, and archived rooms simply stop appearing in search.
